Official IAP Immunization Schedule (0 to 18 Years): Complete Child Vaccine Chart

Dr. Dinesh Mittal, MBBS, DCH
Child Specialist & Pediatrician | Sector 3 Rohini, Delhi

Immunization is one of the most effective and life-saving health interventions in pediatric medicine. In India, the Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) periodically updates evidence-based immunization guidelines to provide comprehensive protection against over 20 vaccine-preventable bacterial and viral diseases from birth through adolescence.

Age-Wise IAP Immunization Chart

Below is the standard, age-by-age vaccine schedule recommended by the IAP for children in India:

Meningococcal Vaccine: When & Why is it Needed in Delhi?

Meningococcal disease (caused by Neisseria meningitidis) is a severe, rapidly progressing bacterial infection that causes bacterial meningitis and life-threatening bloodstream infections (meningococcemia). Historically, Delhi NCR and northern Indian cities have experienced sporadic clusters of meningococcal outbreaks.

  • Who Should Receive the Vaccine (Quadrivalent Conjugate - MCV4)?
    • Children entering crowded boarding schools, residential hostels, or university dormitories.
    • Students planning international education (USA, UK, Canada, Australia) where MCV4 is mandatory.
    • Children with high-risk health conditions (e.g., absence of spleen, complement deficiencies, or primary immunodeficiency).
    • During recognized urban outbreak alerts in Delhi NCR.
  • Vaccine Type: The Quadrivalent Meningococcal Conjugate Vaccine (MenACWY / MCV4) provides robust, long-lasting immunity and reduces nasopharyngeal carrier rates.

Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) for Rabies in Children

Rabies is a 100% fatal viral disease, and children are disproportionately vulnerable to stray animal bites (dogs, cats, monkeys) because of outdoor play and lower height (bites often occur around the face or hands).

  • Why Consider Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)?
    • Eliminates the Need for Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG): If an unvaccinated child suffers a severe bite (Category III), costly and scarce Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) is mandatory. If the child has received PrEP, no RIG is ever required.
    • Simpler Post-Bite Treatment: A vaccinated child requires only 2 simple booster shots (on Day 0 and Day 3) if bitten in the future, with zero need for the full 4 or 5-dose series.
    • Protects Against Unreported Scratches: Toddlers often forget to tell parents about minor licks or scratches from stray puppies. PrEP provides continuous background antibodies.
  • IAP/WHO PrEP Schedule: Administered as 2 doses intramuscularly (on Day 0 and Day 7) in the deltoid or anterolateral thigh.

Practical Pediatric Tips for Parents

Essential Vaccination Rules:
  • Managing Post-Vaccine Mild Fever: Mild fever ($99.5^\circ\text{F}–101^\circ\text{F}$) and local soreness are normal signs that the immune system is creating antibodies. Administer pediatrician-prescribed paracetamol only if the child is uncomfortable. Do not give paracetamol preventively before the injection.
  • Never Restart a Delayed Schedule (Catch-Up Rules): If your child missed a vaccine dose due to travel or mild illness, do not restart from dose 1. Simply continue from the missed dose with your pediatrician's guidance.
  • Cold-Chain Maintenance: Always receive vaccines from established pediatric clinics that utilize specialized medical refrigerators ($+2^\circ\text{C}$ to $+8^\circ\text{C}$) with continuous digital temperature loggers.
  • Keep the Physical & Digital Record Safe: Retain your child's physical vaccination card for school admissions, college documentation, and international travel health clearance.
