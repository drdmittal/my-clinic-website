Parent's Ultimate Guide to Breastfeeding, Weaning & Age-Wise Nutrition (0–18 Years)

Designed specifically for Delhi NCR families. Explore authentic medical guidelines, expert solutions to feeding challenges, and customizable meal plans for Pure Veg, Jain, Eggetarian, and Non-Veg households.

Mandatory Medical Disclaimer

This guide is for educational and informational purposes only and does not substitute for professional pediatric consultation, diagnosis, or personalized in-person clinical evaluation in Rohini, Delhi. Always consult your qualified pediatrician before introducing new foods or modifying your child's diet.

Key Nutritional Principles:

Initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth. Colostrum (first golden milk) provides crucial natural immunity. Feed on demand (8 to 12 times in 24 hours) without scheduled intervals.

Common Challenges & Delhi Household Solutions:

  • Perceived Low Milk Supply: Address through frequent latching, proper hydration, and traditional North Indian galactagogues like roasted ajwain water, methi seed tea, and gond ladoos (in moderation after consulting doctor).
  • Latch Soreness: Correct infant positioning (asymmetrical latch, tummy-to-tummy) and apply expressed breastmilk or medical-grade lanolin ointment.

Feeding Principles & Milestones:

Start with 2-3 spoonfuls 2 times a day, gradually increasing to 3-4 meals. Foods must be thick and mashed (not watery purees). Continue breastfeeding on demand.

North Indian Dietary Matrix (By Preference):

Pure & Jain Veg

Moong dal khichdi (well-cooked and mashed), mashed boiled sweet potato (shakarkandi without root spices if Jain), mashed banana, stewed apple, ragi porridge cooked in water or breast milk.

Eggetarian & Non-Veg

Well-mashed hard-boiled egg yolk (in small quantities after testing tolerance), well-strained soft chicken or bone broth soup without spices.

Milestones & Frequency:

Provide 3-4 meals plus 1-2 snacks per day. Foods can be finely chopped or minced. Encourage self-feeding with soft finger foods to foster motor skills.

Dietary Adaptations:

Soft idli pieces, mashed paneer bhurji (low salt), steamed carrot sticks, well-cooked soft dalia. For non-veg: tender shredded chicken pieces or soft boiled egg white/yolk.

Core Guidance:

Cow's milk can be introduced as a drink (up to 400-500ml max per day). Avoid excessive milk consumption which leads to toddler anorexia and iron-deficiency anemia (a common challenge in North Indian households).

Meal Plan Ideas:

Soft chapati soaked in dal/curry, vegetable paratha bits with ghee, paneer cubes, boiled moong dal cheela, and seasonal fruits like papaya and banana.

Managing Picky Eating:

Preschoolers experience slower growth spurts and natural neophobia (fear of new foods). Avoid forcing food. Present foods creatively (e.g., beetroot paratha for color, vegetable cutlets shaped like stars).

Balanced North Indian Thali:

Dal (protein), phulka with ghee (energy), seasonal vegetable sabzi (micronutrients), curd or paneer (calcium), and fresh salads.

Nutritional Focus:

High physical activity and school study demands require sustained release carbohydrates (whole wheat, oats), iron-rich greens (palak, methi), and calcium for bone mineralization.

Healthy Tiffin Ideas (Delhi School Friendly):

Paneer stuffed missi roti, vegetable poha, idli with coconut/peanut chutney, sprouts chaat, or wholesome besan chilla.

Critical Requirements:

Adolescence is the second largest period of rapid physical growth. Adequate protein, calcium, zinc, and iron (especially vital for teenage girls to prevent anemia) are essential. Limit processed junk foods and sugary beverages.

Dietary Recommendations:

Sprouts, paneer, curd, pulses, nuts (almonds and walnuts), green leafy vegetables, eggs or lean chicken/fish for non-vegetarians, combined with regular outdoor sports.
