Diarrhoea and ORS at home दस्त और घर पर ORS

Loose, watery stools are usually caused by a viral infection. Most children get better on their own in 3 to 7 days. The main job at home is to stop your child from becoming dry (dehydrated).

संक्षेप में In short

  • हर पतले दस्त के बाद ORS दें, छोटे-छोटे घूँट में।
  • खाना और दूध जारी रखें।
  • बिना डॉक्टर की सलाह के दवा न दें।
  • नीचे दिए खतरे के संकेत दिखें तो तुरंत डॉक्टर को दिखाएँ।

Fluids at home घर पर पानी और ORS

  • Give ORS after every loose stool or vomit. Give it in small sips or spoonfuls for babies, and a cup at a time for older children.
  • Use the ORS packet only as printed on it, mixed with clean or boiled water that has cooled.
  • If ORS is not available, give plain clean water, dal water, rice water or coconut water, along with breast milk or normal fluids.
  • Keep breastfeeding. Continue the usual milk and food. Do not starve your child.
  • Zinc tablets are often advised for diarrhoea. Give them only as your doctor prescribes.

Please avoid इन्हें न दें

  • Anti-diarrhoea medicines, antibiotics or any other medicine unless your doctor has advised it.
  • Fruit juice, cola, sweet drinks, or tea. They can make the loose stools worse.
  • Stopping milk or food for a long time.
  • Home remedies you have not checked with the doctor.

Keep clean साफ़-सफ़ाई

  • Wash your hands with soap after toilet, after changing nappies, and before food.
  • Use boiled or filtered water. Keep food covered.
  • Diarrhoea spreads easily. Keep your child away from other children until the stools settle.

See the doctor now if अभी डॉक्टर को दिखाएँ

  • Blood or black colour in the stools.
  • Vomiting again and again, or cannot keep fluids down.
  • Very sleepy, floppy, or not responding normally.
  • Dry mouth, no tears while crying, or sunken eyes.
  • No wee for about 6 hours (in babies, no wet nappy for 6 hours).
  • Very thirsty and refuses fluids.
  • Any diarrhoea in a baby under 3 months.
  • High fever, or fever that lasts more than 2 to 3 days.
  • Severe belly pain or a very swollen belly.
  • Diarrhoea that lasts more than 14 days, or your child is getting weaker.

Do not wait. If you are worried, call the clinic or come in. It is always better to check early.
